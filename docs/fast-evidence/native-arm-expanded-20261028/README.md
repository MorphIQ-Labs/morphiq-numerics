## Workloads and decision rule

Frozen `banks.json` and `cells.json` contain exact binary64 inputs and classifications declared before timing. Ordinary slice lengths are 0–17, 31–33, 51–53, 63–65, 256, 4,096 and 65,536. Additional banks cover a wide accelerated domain, reduction/table boundaries and their adjacent floats, tails/cancellation, one-in-eight fallback mixtures, fallback-only values, subnormal/overflow regions and special values. The 163 cells comprise 90 ordinary slice comparisons, 28 scalar/bank comparisons, 36 additional slice/bank comparisons, four dependent-latency pairs, three cache diagnostics and two composition diagnostics. Input populations and fallback selection are retained separately.

Each cell has ten fresh processes in two blocks of five, a 30-second block gap and two alternating paired rounds per process. Independent per-implementation counts are calibrated to 350 ms then frozen; half-count excluded warmups precede timed observations. Every timed observation must be at least 250 ms. The uncertainty unit is the process: median of its two paired log ratios, then a one-sided Student-t bound across ten processes (nine degrees of freedom). Every required cell must pass independently; the campaign stops after a completed group if any required cost or duration criterion fails. Critical cells were measured first (21), then the remaining 142. Calibration, warmups, all raw timings and host snapshots remain in the archive.

Required comparisons contain accelerated inputs and the declared practical comparable references. Empty calls, fallback/special populations, cache and composition observations were diagnostic from the outset. The guarantees are not identical: CORE-MATH exp is correctly rounded; SLEEF exp uses u10 and erfc uses u15; libcerf, SciPy and composed SLEEF are pinned practical references with independently observed accuracy. All six reference interfaces meet the production fast error budgets on the sampled accelerated banks; that observation does not establish their whole-domain bounds, cross-target identity or matching special semantics.

Diagnostic losses are retained: mixed and fallback-heavy exp/erfcx workloads can be substantially slower because they delegate to the existing strong APIs. Exp fallback slices take about 25–26 times the selected reference time on these adversarial banks. The four-pass CDF composition is about 20–23% slower than the public fused CDF slice. These are explicit workload limitations, not parity claims for every possible input population. Strong fallback performance and consumer end-to-end choices remain separate contract decisions.

Verify `SHA256SUMS` and extract each archive into a fresh scratch directory. No external reference source, library binaries, venv or build products are bundled. Build the pinned references using `manifest.json`; the initial ARM packet contains their original build logs. Rebuild the archived `campaign/wrapper` against the named clean Numerics checkout after adjusting its recorded absolute dependency path. Use the pinned Python 3.14.10 packages listed in the manifest.
